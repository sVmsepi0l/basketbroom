## 1. one-page overview

**basketbroom is an eight-a-side north american arena broomsport built around simultaneous scoring attacks, weaponized rebounds, vertical recovery, legal wandplay, and two chase-ball pressures.** sixteen riders operate amid as many as seven live balls while elastic perimeter nets and a continuous trampoline floor turn the venue into part of the offense. it is severe but fun: organized arena chaos with rules sturdy enough to survive contact.

each team fields one **netminder**, two **chasers**, one **trapper**, one **ranger**, two **hurleybacks**, and one **Scout**. the ranger shifts among chaser-like offense, defensive safety, and secondary scout work. hurleybacks use shallow-pocketed striking sticks called **hurleys** to catch, cradle, sling, redirect, and bank bludgers without monopolizing them.

the pitch measures **420 feet goal to goal**. rebound nets surround the footprint, the roof remains open, and the continuous elastic floor returns loose balls and fallen riders vertically. at each end stand three large hoops specified as **69 feet high**, plus one small hoop above the center large hoop.

| ball | Qty. | primary action | result |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| quaffle | 1 | shoot through any large hoop | 13 points |
| quark | 2 | shoot through the small hoop | 37 points |
| snipe | 1 | catch; then remove for 3 minutes and re-release | 69 points |
| snitch | 1 | catch; ends the match | 150 regulation / 300 overtime |
| bludger | 2 | strike, sling, redirect, or bank with a hurley | disruption; no points |

the quaffle and quarks are **carried scoring balls**; no player may control more than one. the snipe and snitch are **chase balls**, while the bludgers are non-scoring **hazard balls**.

regulation is **four 44-minute quarters**. a legal snitch catch awards 150 and ends the match. the higher certified score wins; if the award creates an exact tie, the catching team wins the alpha capture tiebreak. the catching team can still lose if it remains behind. without a catch, a regulation lead of **150 or more** wins; a margin from 0 through 149 sends the match to one **22-minute overtime**. the capture tiebreak is **needs playtest**.

overtime carries the score forward with all seven balls active. the snitch is worth **300** and still ends the match; a verified **150-point lead** also ends overtime. at the horn, any nonzero lead wins. only a tied overtime without a snitch catch advances to a **Donnybrook**.

in a donnybrook, only the snipe and both quarks remain. positions are suspended, hurleys are removed, and every legally available rider—up to eight per team—may play either surviving ball class. the first legal snipe catch or first legal quark score wins. the official signal is: **“let’s have a Donnybrook.”**

there is no top net. under the **no crown rule**, a non-winged ball crossing the roofline becomes individually dead, is promptly returned from its mapped exit point, and creates a penalty enforced at the next stoppage. other balls continue. deliberately using the roof to erase a threat is **dead-roof Delay**.

wandplay is legal, but sporting integrity and bodily safety outrank spectacle. lethal or permanently injuring magic, mind or memory interference, possession or soul magic, protected-anatomy attacks, broom sabotage, ball teleportation, and interference with official ball identity or function are forbidden.

the hurley is an original basketbroom tool informed separately by gaelic hurling/camogie and by lacrosse and distinct indigenous north american stickball traditions. those living traditions require accurate attribution and paid consultation, not interchangeable costume language.

## 2. team positions

each team fields exactly eight active players.

| position | count | primary assignment | legal ball interaction | hard limit |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| **netminder** | 1 | guards all four hoops; initiates clears and counterattacks. | may catch, carry, pass, and shoot the quaffle and Quarks. | has no special authority over chase balls or Bludgers. protected scoring-ball restarts are its only basketbroom-specific privilege in this alpha. |
| **chaser** | 2 | creates lanes, advances carried scoring balls, and shoots. | may catch, carry, pass, and shoot the quaffle and Quarks. | may control only one carried scoring ball at a time. |
| **trapper** | 1 | marks carriers, closes lanes, intercepts, strips, and forces bad releases. | may intercept, carry, pass, clear, and shoot the quaffle and Quarks. | “defensive specialist” is a tactical assignment, not an unrestricted checking license. allowing the trapper to shoot is the alpha default; **needs playtest**. |
| **ranger** | 1 | switches among chaser-like scorer, defensive safety, and secondary scout according to game state. | may handle the quaffle and quarks and may legally catch the snipe or Snitch. | must clearly release any carried scoring ball before beginning a legal chase-ball catch. safety is a mode, not a ninth position. |
| **hurleyback** | 2 | controls bludger traffic, protects teammates, disrupts attacks, and creates banked hazards. | may strike, tip, catch, cradle, sling, pass, redirect, and bank one bludger using a legal Hurley. | may not secure a bludger with hand, body, broom, or wand; may not individually control two bludgers; obeys the three-count release and team relay rules. |
| **scout** | 1 | tracks, pursues, and catches both chase balls. | may make legal snipe and snitch catches. | may not possess a quaffle or quark or intentionally play a Bludger. |

### 2.1 ball-control classes

#### carried scoring balls

the quaffle and two quarks may be held, carried, passed, and shot. a player establishes control when a ball is secured in a hand, trapped against the body or broom, or otherwise travels under that player's continuous direction. a brief tip, block, bobble, or involuntary contact is not control.

no player may control two carried scoring balls at once. a player controlling one may make a single protective deflection of another but may not catch, pin, tether, shepherd, or repeatedly redirect it.

#### chase balls

the snipe and snitch are captured, not carried. a legal catch resolves immediately: the snipe enters timeout; the snitch terminates regulation or overtime. a ranger carrying a scoring ball cannot complete a chase-ball catch until the carried ball has clearly left the ranger's control.

#### hazard balls

the two bludgers may be deliberately controlled only by hurleybacks using inspected Hurleys. hurley control does not count against the carried-scoring-ball limit, but it has its own individual and team clocks.

#### unauthorized contact

except during donnybrook, only the positions listed above may deliberately establish control of a ball class. donnybrook suspends positions and expressly authorizes every legally available rider to play either quarks or the Snipe. in normal play, incidental or reflexive protective contact is not a foul, but an ineligible player must let the ball go immediately and may not turn the contact into a pass, shot, catch, pin, or tactical redirection. the clean alpha rule is that scouts and hurleybacks may deflect but not possess carried scoring balls; **needs playtest**.

### 2.2 position state and reckoner

position designations remain fixed during live play. the ranger's tactical shifts do not constitute a formal position change. formal swaps occur only during an authorized substitution or stoppage; whether swaps are permitted at every stoppage or only between quarters is **needs playtest**.

**reckoner** is not a playing position. it is the title and match-record mark awarded to the rostered scout whose legal snitch catch ends the match after score certification. a ranger may legally make the same catch but does not earn reckoner unless formally serving as scout after an authorized position change.

## 3. equipment

### 3.1 regulation inventory

a regulation match uses:

- sixteen certified active-player brooms;
- two inspected hurleys per team in active play, plus inspected replacements off-pitch;
- one quaffle, two quarks, one snipe, one snitch, and two bludgers;
- four scoring hoops at each end: three large hoops and one small hoop above the center large hoop;
- perimeter rebound nets with an open roof;
- a continuous trampoline floor;
- certified player protection and registered wands; and
- synchronized score, clock, catch, ball-state, roofline, penalty, and replay systems.

### 3.2 the arena as equipment

the arena is an active system rather than scenery.

- **Pitch:** 420 feet goal to goal. width and corner geometry are **needs playtest**.
- **rebound nets:** elastic nets surround the playable footprint. a legal net contact does not make a player or ball out. bank passes, bank shots, evasive rider rebounds, and hurleyback bludger banks are legal.
- **Floor:** the entire floor is a continuous trampoline surface with a uniform, certified response. a loose ball remains live after a floor bounce. a rider may use a controlled rebound to recover or change elevation, but may not spear, pin, or launch another rider into it.
- **open roof:** there is no crown net. the roofline plane is instrumented for no crown calls. winged chase balls may cross it subject to the aerial chase envelope; non-winged balls may not.
- **goal array:** each end has three large hoops, currently specified as 69 feet high, and a small hoop above the center large hoop. “69 feet high” must be locked as rim height, center height, or total support height. hoop diameters, spacing, small-hoop elevation, and supports are **needs playtest**.
- **safety systems:** hoop supports and net anchors are padded and snag-resistant. emergency descent and fall-arrest systems back up the trampoline floor; the floor alone is not the safety plan.

the floor and net rebound coefficients, net height, playable altitude, aerial chase envelope, and re-entry marks materially determine tactics and are **needs playtest**.

#### executable alpha geometry — **needs playtest**

the first digital/tabletop geometry pass uses these provisional dimensions so that “undefined” does not become an accidental rule:

| element | alpha value |
|---|---|
| pitch footprint | 420 ft long × 210 ft wide |
| rebound-net / roofline height | 138 ft above the trampoline floor |
| ordinary playable altitude | floor through the 138-ft roofline |
| aerial chase envelope | same 420 × 210-ft footprint, extending to 207 ft above the floor |
| large hoops | three 22-ft inner-diameter hoops; centers 69 ft above the floor and 35 ft apart laterally |
| small hoop | 13-ft inner diameter; centered laterally over the center large hoop with its center 100 ft above the floor |
| rebound response | 75% retained incident speed for a clean normal impact, subject to angle and spin |

the 69-ft figure is interpreted here as large-hoop center height, not permanent canon. hoop supports remain outside the clean scoring planes and are padded and bank-live.

### 3.3 basketbroom hurleys

the formal name is **basketbroom hurley**, shortened to **hurley** in play. it has no prongs or tines. “forked” describes only a branch in equipment-development lineage, in the software sense; it is never the equipment's name or shape.

#### separate, credited lineages

the hurley takes different functions from different living sporting traditions:

- a broad, solid striking face and direct striking logic informed by irish hurling and camogie;
- an open pocket and catch–cradle–sling logic informed by lacrosse and distinct indigenous north american stickball traditions; and
- shallow retention, aerial balance, bank play, and a hard possession clock developed specifically for Basketbroom.

these sources are not historically interchangeable. published lore must not reduce indigenous sports to “tribal” flavor, borrow sacred or ceremonial imagery as decoration, or claim the basketbroom tool is an authentic irish or indigenous stick. attribute only specific influences that can be supported, employ paid cultural and sporting consultants before publication, and preserve the names and differences of any traditions discussed.

#### provisional construction standard

| component | alpha standard |
|---|---|
| overall form | one shaft ending in an asymmetric head with a solid lower striking face and an open, shallow upper pocket. no hook, tine, spike, or sharpened edge. |
| length | 36–42 in. / 91–107 cm. **needs playtest** for reach, leverage, and rider safety. |
| head width | approximately 1.15–1.35 bludger diameters at the widest legal point. **needs playtest**. |
| striking face | reinforced and visibly marked. it may be flat or gently curved but cannot capture a rider, broom, net, or hoop. |
| pocket | flexible, visibly open, and sized for one Bludger. with the ball seated, its top must remain above the sidewall plane; the ball can never be concealed. |
| release test | when the head is slowly inverted past 90 degrees, a regulation bludger must fall free without shaking. adhesive, cinching, closing, or magically deepening pockets fail inspection. |
| shaft | non-telescoping oval or mildly flattened grip with a padded butt. no concealed wand, projectile, blade, or retention mechanism. |
| edges | rounded and energy-absorbing; all joints and corners pass the league impact gauge. |
| flex and mass | must fall within a homologation band. exact limits and balance point are **needs playtest**. |
| enchantment | passive durability, visibility, identification, and standardized impact damping only. no attraction, propulsion, aim correction, adhesion, dimensional change, or autonomous movement. |
| retention | no wrist tether or non-breakaway lanyard. a standardized breakaway grip or coupling is **needs playtest**. |

every hurley receives a physical gauge test and magical scan before play. pocket depth, flex, enchantment, and head integrity are rechecked after replacement or major collision.

#### legal techniques

a hurleyback may:

- strike or tip a free bludger with the designated striking face;
- receive one free bludger into the pocket;
- cradle briefly to stabilize it;
- sling, push-pass, or redirect it into free flight;
- pass it to the other hurleyback;
- bank it off a rebound net or trampoline floor; and
- make a controlled stick-on-stick challenge against an opponent's hurley head or shaft below the opponent's lowest hand.

hurley control is never protected possession. an opponent may challenge immediately within the legal equipment-contact envelope.

#### three-count release rule — **needs playtest**

a hurleyback may maintain secured control of one bludger for no more than **3.0 continuous seconds**.

the clock begins at the earliest of these events:

1. the pocket supports the bludger rather than merely touching it;
2. the player completes one deliberate cradle;
3. the ball travels with the hurley against its head or sidewall; or
4. deliberate hurley-to-ball contact lasts longer than 0.5 seconds.

a strike, tip, or one-touch redirection that separates within 0.5 seconds does not start the clock. switching hands, rotating the hurley, brushing the ball against rider or broom, or briefly loosening the pocket does not reset it.

a valid release occurs only when the ball completely clears the head with no physical or magical attachment. if the same hurleyback re-secures a self-toss before it travels 10 feet / 3 metres or contacts another player, hurley, net, floor, or goal structure, the original count continues. arena contact resets the clock only if the ball enters genuinely contestable free flight; a private self-bank does not.

at two seconds, the visible control indicator warns. at 3.0 seconds, the official calls **illegal prolonged bludger control**, neutralizes that bludger, and awards it to the nearest opposing hurleyback for an uncontested restart. repetition or obvious intent escalates the sanction.

#### team relay rule — **needs playtest**

a clean pass begins the receiving hurleyback's individual three-count, but one team may not maintain uninterrupted secured control of the same bludger for more than **6.0 cumulative seconds**. team control resets only when an opponent touches or challenges the bludger, or the ball spends at least one second in contestable free flight after clearing a player, net, floor, or goal structure. short handoffs, token taps, and rehearsed self-banks do not reset the team count.

this six-second layer exists to stop two hurleybacks from laundering possession through each other's pockets. its duration and reset test are **needs playtest**.

#### illegal hurley acts

it is illegal to:

- intentionally touch the quaffle, a quark, the snipe, or the snitch with a hurley;
- secure a bludger by hand, clothing, body, broom, wand, or pocket-altering magic;
- individually control or trap both bludgers;
- throw or deliberately release the hurley;
- hook, clamp, tow, strike, or pin a player, broom, wand, hoop, or net;
- check an opponent's hands, arms, head, neck, torso, or broom;
- swing through an opponent after legal hurley-to-hurley contact;
- grab, close, tangle, enchant, or tamper with a pocket;
- brace on the floor or net to over-flex the shaft or catapult a bludger;
- spear the floor with the shaft or butt;
- release from a closed, illegally deepened, or magically tensioned pocket; or
- use an uninspected replacement.

the exact force limit and legal checking envelope are **needs playtest**. until measured, only controlled contact with opposing equipment—not the opponent—is legal.

### 3.4 brooms

base broom-sport certification applies unless modified here. every broom must:

- fall within league acceleration, speed, braking, and turning bands;
- carry standardized emergency-descent and rider-protection systems independent of the player's wand;
- have padded contact points and no cutting, piercing, or snagging hardware;
- contain no ball compartment, active weapon, targeting system, or spell emitter;
- bear a visible inspection seal; and
- be replaced only by an inspected broom in the same approved class.

a broom may not trap a ball against the floor, net, hoop, or player. no modification may defeat the floor, rebound nets, ball rules, or open roof.

### 3.5 protection and uniforms

required protection includes a certified helmet with eye and facial protection, mouthguard, throat and cervical protection, gloves, flexible rib/sternum/spinal padding, hip/knee/shin protection, and extra hand/forearm protection for Hurleybacks.

uniforms must show team, number, and active position clearly enough for officials to identify ball authority. loose capes, jewelry, rigid ornament, dangling straps, and non-breakaway cords are prohibited. passive protective enchantments must be standardized and disclosed; concealing, retaliatory, performance-enhancing, or ball-affecting enchantments are illegal.

### 3.6 wands

each player may carry one registered and inspected wand in a close-fitting holster. a wand may not be built into a broom or hurley; used as a brace, striking tool, or ball-retention extension; equipped with automated casting or targeting; or secured by a dangling non-breakaway tether. legal spell effects are governed by section 6.

### 3.7 official systems

officials require synchronized systems for:

- regulation, overtime, removal, and snipe-timeout clocks;
- visible individual and team hurley-control counts;
- goal and chase-catch validation;
- ball identity and live/dead state;
- roofline exits and mapped re-entry coordinates;
- equipment and enchantment inspection;
- delayed-penalty logging; and
- recording reckoner and other match distinctions.

## 4. balls

### 4.1 official ball set

basketbroom uses seven official balls in three functional classes.

| ball | Qty. | class and form | eligible deliberate interaction | scoring / effect | reset |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|
| **quaffle** | 1 | carried scoring ball; non-winged; reference size | netminder, chasers, trapper, ranger | 13 points through a large hoop only | standard scoring-ball restart after a goal |
| **quark** | 2 | carried scoring ball; non-winged; each approximately two-thirds the quaffle's diameter | netminder, chasers, trapper, ranger | 37 points through the small hoop only | each quark restarts independently after a goal |
| **snipe** | 1 | copper, self-propelled chase ball; slightly larger than the snitch; about 60% of snitch speed | scout or ranger | 69 points per legal catch | dead for 3 minutes of live match time, then re-released |
| **snitch** | 1 | self-propelled chase ball; winged | scout or ranger | 150 in regulation; 300 in overtime; catch ends the match | removed after a verified catch; absent from donnybrook |
| **bludger** | 2 | hazard ball; non-winged | hurleybacks using hurleys | no points; disruption effect follows the base rule pending a standalone basketbroom hit module | remains live after legal strikes, slings, and banks |

ball mass, material, exact diameter, impact ceiling, flight intelligence, visual signature, and acoustic signature—except where already locked above—are **needs playtest**. every ball must be distinguishable by silhouette as well as color so that color vision is never the sole state channel.

the eligibility column governs regulation and overtime. during donnybrook, suspended positions override it: every legally available rider may control one quark or catch the Snipe.

### 4.2 ball-state model

ball state uses three separate fields so that “controlled” never contradicts “live”:

1. **phase status:** `active` or `removed`. a removed ball does not belong to the current match phase.
2. **play state for an active ball:** `live` or `dead`. snipe `timeout` is a recorded reason for being dead, not a third overlapping state.
3. **control state for a live ball:** `free` or `controlled`, with controller and control-clock fields where applicable.

a score, roof exit, foul, review, or global stoppage may make one ball dead while the other active balls remain live. donnybrook marks the quaffle, snitch, and both bludgers removed.

the public scoreboard shows the state of all seven balls. that is essential gameplay information, not broadcast garnish.

### 4.3 regulation release schedule — **needs playtest**

for the first playable alpha:

1. the opening horn releases the quaffle, both quarks, both bludgers, and the Snipe.
2. the snitch releases after **22:00 of live time in the first quarter**.
3. once released, the snitch remains part of regulation through quarter breaks unless caught.
4. a quarter break pauses all live-time clocks. officials secure live balls and re-release them from standard positions at the next horn; an unfinished snipe timeout carries across the break.
5. overtime clears any snipe timeout and begins with all seven balls live.

the 22-minute delayed snitch release guarantees an opening phase in which arena offense can establish the score while preserving the locked overtime rule. it is provisional, not new canon.

### 4.4 hoop scoring

a hoop score is complete when the entire legal ball crosses the plane of the correct hoop from its attacking face to its rear face before that ball or phase becomes dead.

- a quaffle through any of the three large hoops scores 13.
- either quark through the single small hoop scores 37.
- a quaffle through the small hoop or a quark through a large hoop scores nothing; the ball remains live unless an official stops it for safety.
- a legal bank from the rebound net, floor, hoop structure, broom, or another player may score.
- a valid traversal counts for the team attacking that goal, regardless of which team last touched the ball. this is the alpha own-goal rule; **needs playtest**.
- the snipe, snitch, and bludgers can never score by crossing a hoop.

after a verified score, only the scoring ball becomes dead. other ball play continues. for the alpha, the scored ball returns to the defending netminder on the goal axis, 22 feet in front of the goal plane. opponents must stay 13 feet away until the netminder releases the ball or three seconds elapse. exact geometry and protection time are **needs playtest**.

### 4.5 chase-ball catches

a catch may normally be completed only by a scout or ranger who is not controlling a carried scoring ball. during donnybrook, any legally available rider may catch the snipe, still subject to the one-carried-ball limit.

the alpha capture standard requires the eligible player to secure the chase ball by hand for one continuous second while remaining in legal contact with the broom and inside the aerial chase envelope. bobbles, clothing traps, broom traps, wand capture, and contact by an ineligible position do not count. the one-second standard and broom-contact requirement are **needs playtest**.

#### snipe catch cycle

1. a verified catch awards 69 points.
2. the snipe becomes dead immediately and is transferred to the timeout cradle.
3. for the alpha, its three-minute timer counts only live match time; breaks, reviews, and official stoppages pause it. the clock basis is **needs playtest**.
4. at zero, the snipe is re-released from the center launch point 69 feet above the floor. every eligible pursuer must be at least 44 feet away; the release heading is randomized and cannot face either goal directly.
5. a new catch begins a new cycle.

if center is unsafe, the official randomly selects one of four symmetric alternate launch points 44 feet from pitch center at the same altitude. the scoreboard warns at ten seconds. launch geometry, separation, and warning are **needs playtest**.

#### snitch catch cycle

1. a verified regulation catch awards 150 and ends the match.
2. a verified overtime catch awards 300 and ends the match.
3. a catch does not guarantee victory. the awarded points are added, all earlier pending calls are resolved, and the higher certified score wins.
4. if the award produces an exact certified tie, the catching team wins under the alpha capture tiebreak. the tiebreak is **needs playtest**.

a rostered scout whose verified catch ends the match after certification earns the reckoner match mark, even if the final score leaves the scout's team behind.

### 4.6 winged-ball boundary

the snipe and snitch are the only winged balls. they may cross the open roofline and remain live inside a marked aerial chase envelope. eligible riders may pursue them into that envelope. if a chase ball leaves the envelope or becomes unreachable, the chase official recalls and neutrally re-releases it without points.

the envelope's footprint, maximum altitude, escape duration, and rider-return procedure are **needs playtest**. no crown penalties do not apply to lawful chase-ball flight.

### 4.7 bludger behavior

bludgers remain live after legal hurley strikes, slings, deflections, and arena banks. they may be caught in a legal hurley pocket subject to the individual and team control clocks. direct wand control is illegal.

the gameplay consequence of a legal bludger hit currently inherits the base broom-sport assumption. a standalone basketbroom edition must define hit validation, forced recovery, immunity windows, friendly fire, and return-to-play without copying inherited prose. those mechanics are **needs playtest**.

### 4.8 event ordering

goals, catches, fouls, and phase-ending thresholds are timestamped by the official system.

1. an event completed before the horn or a verified snitch catch counts.
2. an event completed afterward does not.
3. when two events are genuinely simultaneous within the system's resolution limit, both point events count before the phase-ending condition is evaluated. **needs playtest**.
4. during overtime, simultaneous points are totaled before testing the 150-point lead.
5. a phase-ending call remains provisional until replay and all penalties committed before its timestamp are resolved.

## 5. match flow

### 5.1 match clock and stoppages

regulation and overtime use **live time**. the match clock runs during ordinary play and stops for an official stoppage, injury, equipment failure, score or catch review, penalty administration, quarter break, or unsafe arena state. snipe timeout and temporary-removal clocks also count live time. this clock model is **needs playtest** against a running-clock alternative.

because several balls operate independently, a single ball becoming dead does not automatically stop the match. officials may service and re-release that ball while the others continue when the applicable restart allows it.

### 5.2 pre-match and opening

before the opening horn, officials:

1. confirm eight active players in the required position distribution for each team;
2. inspect brooms, hurleys, protective equipment, uniforms, and wands;
3. verify the identity, behavior, and state tracking of all seven balls;
4. synchronize the match, snipe, hurley-control, removal, and penalty systems; and
5. place players and opening balls at their standard release marks.

for the executable alpha, every rider starts behind the quarter line 105 feet from that team's goal plane. on the centerline, the quaffle begins at pitch center; the quarks begin 35 feet to either lateral side; the bludgers begin 70 feet to either side; and the snipe launches neutrally from 22 feet above center. all six activate on the horn. the later snitch release uses the same center launch axis.

crossing the quarter line or touching a ball before the horn resets the opening. a first false start is minor and gives the opponent a protected quaffle restart at center; repetition escalates. all opening values and the false-start remedy are **needs playtest**.

### 5.3 regulation

| element | regulation rule |
|---|---|
| duration | four quarters of 44:00 live time |
| score at start | 0–0 |
| opening balls | quaffle, two quarks, two bludgers, snipe; delayed snitch schedule is **needs playtest** |
| snitch value | 150 |
| immediate end | verified snitch catch |
| clock-expiry win | no catch and leader ahead by 150 or more |
| overtime trigger | no snitch catch and margin from 0 through 149 |

at each quarter horn, any point or catch completed before the timestamp counts. all remaining live actions become dead. officials administer pending penalties, secure the balls, permit authorized substitutions or position changes, and prepare the next release.

when the fourth-quarter clock reaches zero without a snitch catch:

- a margin of 150 or more ends the match for the leader;
- a margin of 1–149 advances to overtime; and
- a tie advances to overtime.

### 5.4 overtime

overtime is one period of **22:00 live time**. the regulation score carries forward. all seven balls are live at the opening horn, including a newly released snipe and Snitch.

overtime ends at the first verified terminating condition:

1. **snitch catch:** award 300, resolve earlier pending penalties, then certify the higher score. an exact tie is won by the catching team under the alpha capture tiebreak; **needs playtest**.
2. **lead threshold:** after any verified point event or simultaneous event group, a team leads by 150 or more. that team wins immediately.
3. **clock expiry:** after pending calls are resolved, any nonzero lead wins. a tie advances to Donnybrook.

the 150-point threshold uses the net certified score after all events sharing the terminating timestamp. a merely displayed lead does not end play until scoring validation finishes.

### 5.5 donnybrook

a donnybrook occurs only when the certified score is tied after overtime.

the head official signals: **“let’s have a Donnybrook.”**

#### state change

- active balls: the snipe and both Quarks.
- removed balls: quaffle, snitch, and both Bludgers.
- Positions: suspended.
- active players: every legally available rider—up to eight per team—may carry either quark or catch the snipe; a rider still removed or ejected remains unavailable.
- Equipment: brooms, protection, and registered wands remain; hurleys are removed to the inspected racks because no legal hurley ball remains. keeping or using one is donnybrook equipment Misuse.
- point totals: remain tied until the winning event; for the alpha, the winner receives the normal 69-point snipe credit or 37-point quark credit in the final score. final-score display treatment is **needs playtest**.

#### start and victory

for the alpha start, all riders line up at their own goal line, both quarks begin at equidistant center marks, and the snipe releases neutrally at the horn. geometry and first-contact spacing are **needs playtest**.

the first verified event wins:

- a legal snipe catch; or
- a legal quark score through the attacking small hoop.

there is no donnybrook clock in the alpha and no 150-point threshold. the no-clock format is **needs playtest**. a quaffle goal, snitch catch, bludger action, or use of suspended positional privilege is impossible or illegal because those objects and privileges are absent.

if the timing system cannot distinguish opposing winning events, both are voided, the score returns to the pre-event tie, and all three donnybrook balls receive a neutral reset. the simultaneous-event procedure is **needs playtest**.

wandplay remains subject to the ordinary prohibitions. whether competition donnybrooks should instead be no-wand is **needs playtest**.

### 5.6 no crown rule

the roof is open, but it is not a disposal chute.

#### covered balls

the quaffle, both quarks, and both bludgers are non-winged and covered by no Crown. the snipe and snitch use the winged-ball boundary instead.

#### exit and delayed enforcement

when a covered ball crosses the roofline plane:

1. that ball alone becomes dead at the instant of crossing. other balls remain live.
2. the crown official records the three-dimensional **crown mark**, identifies responsibility, and displays a pending penalty.
3. as soon as safe, the same verified ball is neutrally reintroduced from the crown mark or the nearest legal point directly beneath it. the alpha target is three seconds; **needs playtest**. the reintroduced ball becomes live while the penalty remains pending.
4. the penalty is enforced at the next stoppage. the exit alone does not stop the other balls or the clock.
5. if safe re-entry cannot occur within the target, the crown official calls a safety stoppage, returns the ball, and administers the now-due penalty.

responsibility attaches to the last deliberate propulsive act that materially directed the ball through the crown. a slight incidental graze does not transfer responsibility; a deliberate redirection does. if replay cannot identify a materially responsible act, the re-entry is neutral and no penalty is assessed.

for an ordinary exit, the base sanction is Minor:

- a carried scoring ball is awarded to an eligible opponent at the restart mark; or
- a bludger is given to the nearest opposing hurleyback for an uncontested free-flight restart.

if the nonoffending team obtains an equivalent or better advantage from the neutral re-entry, its captain may decline the restorative possession at the stoppage, but the foul remains recorded for escalation.

deliberately launching, carrying, binding, or repeatedly deflecting a ball through the crown to consume time, erase a threat, or prevent contest is **dead-roof delay**, beginning at moderate and escalating for repetition or strategic denial.

### 5.7 ending and certification

a snitch catch, overtime lead threshold, or final horn ends live play but does not erase fouls already committed. officials review the terminating event, validate all earlier simultaneous scores, enforce pending penalties whose conduct predates the termination timestamp, and then certify the score.

if a pre-termination penalty includes a free or penalty shot, that remedy is completed before the winner is certified. officials then retest the applicable ending rule:

- **snitch catch:** the match remains ended. the higher certified score wins; an exact tie goes to the catching team under the alpha capture tiebreak.
- **regulation horn without a snitch catch:** a margin of 150 or more wins; a margin of 0–149 advances to overtime.
- **overtime 150-point threshold:** if the adjusted margin remains at least 150, the leader wins. if it falls below 150 and time remains, overtime resumes from the termination timestamp.
- **overtime horn without a snitch catch:** any nonzero adjusted lead wins; a tie advances to Donnybrook.

if a snitch catch is overturned, play resumes in the applicable phase with a neutral snitch release and the time restored to the catch timestamp.

## 6. fouls and penalties

basketbroom-specific fouls supplement the base sport's ordinary contact and conduct rules. officials judge the act, danger, advantage gained, intent, repetition, and effect on a scoring or match-ending opportunity.

### 6.1 advantage and delayed enforcement

multi-ball play should not collapse into a whistle every three seconds.

- dangerous serious, severe, or catastrophic conduct stops play immediately.
- a lesser foul may be signaled while the nonoffending team retains meaningful advantage.
- if the offending team controls the affected ball or the advantage disappears, the official stops play and applies the remedy.
- a captain may decline a restorative possession or free shot when live advantage produced a better result.
- removal, ejection, match report, equipment seizure, and score nullification cannot be declined.
- a score created directly by illegal conduct is void. an unrelated legal score completed before the foul stands.

### 6.2 penalty ladder

| level | standard sanction | administration |
|---|---|---|
| **minor** | possession change or protected restart | usually delayed unless the affected ball is already dead |
| **moderate** | free shot or scoring-ball possession award | immediate when needed to restore a denied attack |
| **serious** | penalty shot plus temporary removal | global stoppage; safety review |
| **severe** | ejection plus match report | offender cannot return; equipment may be seized |
| **catastrophic** | forfeit review and league discipline | immediate safety suspension; result remains provisional |

repeated, tactical, retaliatory, terminal-clock, or outcome-changing conduct may be elevated by one or more levels. one act may satisfy several foul definitions, but officials impose one proportionate restorative package rather than stacking duplicate shots.

### 6.3 restarts and shots

#### possession award

the affected carried scoring ball is placed at the foul location or nearest safe point and awarded to an eligible opponent. defenders remain at least 13 feet away until release or three seconds elapse. these values are **needs playtest**.

for a bludger foul, the equivalent remedy is an uncontested free-flight release to an opposing Hurleyback. for chase-ball interference, no catch points are awarded automatically. a neutral snipe re-release uses the standard 44-foot exclusion. on a protected re-release, the offended team designates one currently eligible pursuer to start 22 feet from the launch point while every other rider remains 44 feet away; protection ends after three seconds or once the snipe travels 44 feet. these values are **needs playtest**. a snitch restart uses the same separation at the 100-foot center launch point.

#### free shot

a moderate foul that denies an attack produces a free shot with the affected quaffle or Quark. the shot begins at the foul point or, if that point is closer than 44 feet to the goal plane, at the corresponding point on the 44-foot arc. the netminder may defend; every other defender remains at least 22 feet from the shooter until release. if no carried scoring ball was involved, the default restorative ball is the Quaffle. distances are **needs playtest**.

#### penalty shot

a serious foul produces a penalty shot plus temporary removal.

1. the global clock and all balls stop.
2. if the foul denied a quaffle or quark chance, that ball type is used; otherwise the quaffle is used.
3. one eligible shooter faces the defending netminder from the central mark 44 feet in front of the goal plane, aligned vertically with the target hoop.
4. no pass, second attempt, hurley, or wandplay is permitted during the shot.
5. a make scores the ball's ordinary value. after a make or miss, the defending netminder restarts that ball.

the 44-foot distance and five-second attempt clock are **needs playtest**.

#### temporary removal and ejection — **needs playtest**

a serious foul removes the offender without replacement for **three minutes of live match time**. the timer pauses during global stoppages and intermissions and carries into overtime. the player returns only through the designated gate on the official's signal. multiple removals may leave a team multiple players short.

a player still serving removal when donnybrook begins remains ineligible until the live-time penalty is converted. for the alpha, any unexpired removal becomes exclusion from the remainder of donnybrook; **needs playtest**.

a severe ejection removes the offender for the match. for the alpha, the team remains short through the current quarter and may fill the position at the next quarter break; an overtime or donnybrook ejection receives no replacement. replacement timing is **needs playtest**.

a catastrophic foul permits immediate suspension. the league, not the on-field scoreboard alone, determines forfeit and further discipline.

### 6.4 basketbroom foul catalogue

| foul | definition | usual level |
|---|---|---|
| **illegal spellcraft** | casting outside the wandplay limits or using otherwise legal magic to create a prohibited advantage | moderate to catastrophic |
| **ball binding** | physically, mechanically, or magically fastening, summoning, tethering, immobilizing, concealing, or sustaining directional control over an official ball | moderate; serious when it denies a clear score or chase |
| **snipe stalling** | retaining the snipe after a catch, delaying its timeout or re-release, trapping it, or forcing it outside the chase envelope | moderate; repeated or decisive conduct serious |
| **snitch suppression** | concealing, caging, disabling, delaying, or preventing the snitch from functioning normally | serious; alteration or destruction Severe/Catastrophic |
| **broom sabotage** | tampering with another rider's broom, controls, enchantments, inspection seal, or safety system | severe; catastrophic when systemic or injurious |
| **net pinning** | holding a person, broom, hurley, or ball against the rebound net beyond incidental contact | moderate; dangerous person-pinning serious |
| **floor spearing** | driving a broom, hurley, wand, or other object into the trampoline floor to puncture it, trip someone, or create an uncontrolled launch | serious; severe when damage or injury results |
| **multi-ball hoarding** | controlling a second carried scoring ball, or using body, broom, equipment, or magic to evade the single-ball rule | minor; deliberate or repeated conduct moderate |
| **wrong-hoop deception** | disguising a ball, hoop, score signal, or attacking direction to manufacture an invalid score | moderate; official-equipment alteration severe |
| **dead-roof delay** | deliberately exploiting the open crown to remove a non-winged ball, erase an attack, or consume time | moderate; decisive or repeated conduct serious |
| **unauthorized ball play** | deliberately establishing control of a ball class not assigned to the player's position | Minor/Moderate; chase-ball interference serious |
| **donnybrook equipment misuse** | using a removed ball, hurley, or suspended positional privilege during donnybrook | moderate; dangerous or outcome-changing use serious |
| **official-ball tampering** | destroying, duplicating, replacing, concealing, or materially altering an official ball | Severe/Catastrophic |

a simple missed attempt through the wrong hoop is not wrong-hoop Deception. it scores zero and remains ordinary play.

### 6.5 hurley-specific fouls

legal hurley contact is directed at a bludger or at an opponent's hurley while both players contest that Bludger. until physical testing establishes a wider envelope, only controlled, visible equipment-to-equipment contact below the opponent's lowest hand is legal.

| hurley foul | definition | usual level |
|---|---|---|
| **illegal prolonged bludger control** | exceeding the individual three-second control clock | minor; repeated violations moderate |
| **team relay hoarding** | exceeding the six-second team clock or laundering control through short pocket-to-pocket relays | Minor/Moderate |
| **control-timer evasion** | self-banking, bobbling, token tapping, hand switching, or sham release intended only to reset a control count | Minor/Moderate |
| **dangerous hook or check** | hooking or checking body, broom, hand, head, neck, or equipment from a blind or unsafe angle | moderate; dangerous contact serious |
| **net-pocket interference** | anchoring or tangling the hurley pocket in a rebound net to trap a ball, equipment, or rider or to create leverage | moderate; person entanglement serious |
| **pocket tampering** | grabbing, closing, enchanting, tangling, or altering an opponent's hurley pocket | moderate; equipment damage serious |
| **illegal sling release** | releasing without control, at a forbidden anatomical target, from an unsafe close range, or through the crown | Moderate/Serious |
| **unsafe hurley contact** | striking, spearing, cross-checking, towing, or swinging through a person or broom rather than playing the bludger | serious; protected-area or injurious contact severe |
| **illegal hurley ball use** | deliberately catching, carrying, striking, or redirecting a quaffle, quark, snipe, or snitch with a hurley | moderate; chase-ball interference serious |
| **hurley throwing** | releasing the hurley as a projectile or abandoning it in a live flight lane | serious; contact or injury severe |
| **bludger pinning** | holding a bludger against body, broom, floor, net, hoop, or other equipment instead of releasing it | minor; deliberate/repeated conduct moderate |
| **illegal hurley modification** | using an uninspected, adhesive, closing, sharpened, autonomous, or otherwise nonconforming hurley | severe; coordinated tampering catastrophic |

safe sling distance, legal check arc, force ceiling, and protected-body radius are **needs playtest**.

### 6.6 wandplay

canon permits wandplay unless a basketbroom prohibition applies. for an executable alpha, a legal cast must also belong to the provisional whitelist below. wandplay never overrides positional ball authority, the one-ball rule, equipment certification, or protected anatomy. the whitelist restriction and every value in it are **needs playtest**.

#### absolutely prohibited

- Unforgivables.
- lethal or permanently injuring magic.
- mind control.
- memory modification.
- possession or soul magic.
- direct attacks to heart, spine, throat, eyes, or nervous system.
- broom sabotage.
- teleporting a ball through a hoop.
- destroying, duplicating, concealing, or altering an official ball.
- preventing the snipe or snitch from functioning.

a teleported ball scores nothing and ordinarily produces a serious penalty. broom sabotage and official-ball alteration are ordinarily Severe. unforgivables, possession or soul magic, and deliberately lethal magic are Catastrophic.

#### executable alpha whitelist — **needs playtest**

| cast | legal target and effect | alpha limit |
|---|---|---|
| **self impulse** | caster only; one visible push, brake, or rotation that does not modify the broom | effect ends within 0.5 seconds; six-second personal cooldown |
| **personal countershield** | caster and mounted broom; blocks one otherwise legal spell but cannot block, trap, or redirect a player or ball | maximum one second; six-second personal cooldown |
| **scoring-ball impulse** | one quaffle or quark the caster is currently eligible to play, including under suspended donnybrook positions; one visible redirection | ball within 10 feet; effect ends within 0.5 seconds; six-second personal cooldown |
| **counterspell** | one visible whitelisted cast currently resolving | instantaneous; same range as the countered cast; six-second personal cooldown |
| **signal spark** | open air; non-obscuring light or sound used for team communication | maximum one second; cannot imitate an official signal or conceal play |

spells targeting an opponent, an opponent's equipment, an arena component, a bludger, the snipe, or the snitch are outside the executable alpha whitelist even when they do not fall into an absolute prohibition.

#### alpha direct-ball rule

an eligible scoring-ball player may apply one visible, instantaneous magical impulse to a quaffle or quark that the player could otherwise legally touch. the effect must end within 0.5 seconds and may redirect but not summon, carry, stop, tether, conceal, transform, or automatically aim the ball. a spell cannot complete the ball's passage through a hoop by teleportation.

hurleybacks may not use wandplay to replace the hurley on a Bludger. no player may cast directly on the snipe or Snitch. sustained or repeated force that gives practical possession is ball Binding.

this narrow impulse rule retains magical tactics without making physical roles ornamental. a later ruleset may expand the whitelist only with explicit target, range, duration, cooldown, counterplay, visual tell, and scoring interaction.

### 6.7 donnybrook enforcement

ordinary safety and conduct rules remain active in Donnybrook.

- a minor carried-ball foul produces protected possession of the affected Quark.
- a moderate denial produces a free quark shot; the defending team designates one goal defender because positions are suspended.
- a serious denial produces a quark penalty shot and removal. a successful shot wins.
- chase-ball interference produces a protected snipe re-release rather than automatic catch points.
- severe or catastrophic conduct may decide the contest only through ejection, safety suspension, and forfeit review; officials do not invent an automatic catch after the fact.

exact donnybrook free-shot geometry is **needs playtest**.

### 8.1 prioritized risk register

| priority | risk | likely failure mode | control or test |

| **p0** | **too close to quidditch** | inherited ball names, analogous specialist roles, hoop scoring, brooms, and a match-ending catch may make the project read as derivative despite the original arena system. | build original possession, movement, contact, arena, and officiating rules; perform a terminology and visual-identity audit; obtain qualified ip review before commercial release. rewritten prose alone is not a moat. |

## 9. prioritized next-step plan

### 9.1 recommended sequence

#### 1. freeze draft 0.1 and write the standalone kernel

treat this bible as the canon-and-issues baseline. next, define original basketbroom rules for broom movement, ordinary contact, substitutions, bludger-hit recovery, restarts, injuries, officiating roles, and whether any protected goal volume should exist. the exit gate is one complete match resolvable without reference to another fictional ruleset.

#### 2. build the tabletop alpha first

tabletop is the fastest and cheapest place to test the genuinely hard questions: seven simultaneous objectives, scoring ratios, role agency, release timing, penalty restoration, and donnybrook balance.

recommended alpha components:

- a 420-foot pitch abstracted into fourteen 30-foot longitudinal zones;
- three altitude bands plus floor and crown states;
- rebound-bank zones along every perimeter edge;
- sixteen role cards, seven ball tokens, control clocks, removal markers, and a public ball-state board;
- one-minute ticks grouped into eleven four-tick rounds per 44-minute quarter; and
- a score track that adds 13, 37, 69, 150, and 300 without mental bookkeeping.

the zone scale, action economy, and tick structure are **needs playtest**.

add systems in this order: quaffle and quarks; positions and defense; rebound nets/floor; bludgers and hurleys; snipe; Snitch/end conditions; penalties; wandplay. run isolated donnybrooks separately before attaching them to full matches.

#### 3. run a scoring and pacing sensitivity pass

vary shot rate, catch rate, possession length, snipe live time, snitch release time, turnover rate, and netminder save rate. the model should identify fragile assumptions, not pretend invented probabilities prove balance. specifically test how many 13-point possessions are needed to answer one quark, snipe, or snitch event and how often snipe totals exceed all hoop offense.

#### 4. build three digital microprototypes

build separately before attempting 8v8:

1. rebound nets, floor response, open roof, crown marks, and goal planes;
2. broom flight, collision, fall, emergency descent, and trampoline recovery; and
3. hurley catch–cradle–sling, individual/team clocks, legal banks, and stick challenges.

each microprototype should emit deterministic event logs. pretty assets can wait; the arena needs to tell the truth before it wears eyeliner.

#### 5. integrate a reduced digital match

begin 3v3 or 4v4 with carried scoring balls only, then add hazard and chase systems one at a time. implement balls as explicit state machines and keep server/host authority over score, catch, crown, timeout, and terminating events. add full positions and 8v8 only when spectators can identify every critical state without developer narration.

#### 6. add controlled wandplay

compare no-spell sessions with the alpha whitelist. keep only magic with a clear target, range, duration, cooldown, counter, visual tell, and event-log effect. reject spells that are compulsory, invisible, dispute-heavy, or functionally replace a position.

#### 7. produce the production/mod bible

translate validated rules into:

- data schemas and state diagrams;
- network authority and rollback rules;
- ai responsibilities by position;
- animation and collision requirements;
- camera, replay, scoreboard, sound, and accessibility systems;
- arena and equipment asset specifications; and
- a versioned canon/decision log.

### 9.3 playtest scorecard

all gates below are starting hypotheses and **needs playtest**.

| question | measurement | provisional gate |
|---|---|---|
| can a new player learn the balls? | after a ten-minute teach, match each ball to legal player, action, hoop/value, and reset. | at least 80% identify all five ball types correctly without prompting. |
| is live play readable? | freeze random moments; ask players/observers for leader, three carried-ball possessions, and chase-ball states. | at least 80% correct within five seconds. |
| is scorekeeping practical? | count arithmetic corrections, missed scores, and delayed updates. | no more than one correction per match; display updates within three seconds. |
| does a route dominate? | record point share from quaffle, quarks, snipe, and Snitch. | no non-snitch route repeatedly supplies over 60% without a deliberate strategic cause. |
| does the snipe overwhelm offense? | record catches, live time per catch, and snipe point share. | revisit behavior if snipe points repeatedly exceed all hoop points combined. |
| is match duration usable? | record real session time and fictional p10, median, and p90 ending time. | tabletop median under 90 minutes; digital/broadcast target set before production. |
| does every role decide? | mark active windows containing at least two materially different legal choices. | each role has a meaningful choice in at least 75% of its active windows. |
| is ranger too strong? | compare touches, points, assists, stops, chase pressure, and leverage by role. | ranger does not lead both offense and defense in most matches. |
| can one netminder cover four hoops? | track shots, angles, difficulty, and saves by hoop. | no hoop is effectively undefendable or strategically irrelevant. |
| is hurley control contestable? | record individual/team duration, disputes, and reset exploits. | zero ambiguous clock rulings in the last two alpha matches; no repeatable laundering loop. |
| is no crown enforceable? | record exits, forgotten flags, restart disputes, and net advantage. | every exit logged; zero forgotten penalties; deliberate exits never pay. |
| is donnybrook balanced? | run at least 30; record first touch, winning route, side, and duration. | neither route exceeds 70% of wins; median under five minutes. |
| are arena banks learned skill? | repeat identical digital releases and compare novice/practiced accuracy. | same input stays within tolerance and practice produces measurable improvement. |
| does wandplay improve play? | compare matched no-spell and whitelist sessions; count disputes, stoppages, and selection. | added decisions outweigh added stoppages; no single spell becomes compulsory. |
